Research

Speech, eye movements, physiological signals, and task performance contain patterns of timing, sequence, and organization. I study how to represent those patterns, what they reveal about human cognition, and when they support reliable measurement.

  1. BehaviorSpeech, gaze, physiological signals, task performance
  2. ModelsStochastic, nonlinear, and AI methods
  3. DiscoveryInterpretable behavioral signatures
  4. MeasurementCognitive and health indicators
  5. TranslationProspective studies and technology

Behavioral dynamics

I develop and adapt stochastic, Markov, and nonlinear representations of speech, gaze, and physiological signals. These models make transitions, recurrence, and temporal organization available for scientific investigation.

Interpretable signatures

I combine these representations with statistical learning and AI to discover candidate biomarkers of cognitive and mental health. A central question is whether a signature reflects the individual, the task, or the way behavior was measured.

Intelligent measurement

I translate supported discoveries into measurement systems and evaluate their reliability in prospective human studies. My future work will develop personalized baselines and adaptive systems that choose informative measurements while making uncertainty explicit.

Current directions

Current projects examine gaze transitions in immersive virtual reality, multifractal organization of visual exploration, and the comparability of speech-timing measures across tasks. The next stage will investigate stability within individuals, complementary information across speech and gaze, and AI-guided task selection for repeated measurement.

Innovation

I founded DementiAnalytics to move speech-based biomarker research toward practical measurement technologies. With clinical and engineering collaborators, I lead ongoing, IRB-approved prospective data collection involving 45 residents and 180+ recordings, with five senior-living communities enrolled. Longitudinal reassessment is planned to study within-person change. My work has also led to five patent applications spanning speech, language, and eye-tracking approaches to cognitive assessment.

About

I am completing my Ph.D. in Industrial Engineering at Penn State, advised by Hui Yang, with graduation expected in May 2027. I previously earned an M.S. in Applied Computational Mathematics and Statistics at the University of Notre Dame and a B.S. in Applied Mathematics with honors (cum laude) from Virginia Tech, where I was also an NCAA Division I athlete. I previously worked in quantitative finance and capital markets at Wells Fargo and Citadel. My work brings systems engineering into collaboration with speech-language pathology, neuropsychology, and other disciplines studying human behavior.

Teaching and mentoring

I have co-taught probability and statistics and supported courses in simulation and engineering analytics. I mentor undergraduate researchers across engineering, computer science, neuroscience, and health sciences. My teaching interests also include time-series analysis, computational modeling, and machine learning.
