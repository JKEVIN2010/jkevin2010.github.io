Research

Speech, eye movements, physiological signals, and task performance contain patterns of timing, sequence, and organization. I study how to represent those patterns, what they reveal about human cognition, and when they support reliable measurement.

Behavioral dynamics

Stochastic, Markov, and nonlinear models that expose the timing and organization of speech, gaze, and physiological signals.

Interpretable signatures

Candidate biomarkers of cognitive and mental health, tested for whether they reflect the person, the task, or the measurement.

Intelligent measurement

Prospective studies, personal baselines, and adaptive AI that chooses the most informative next measurement.

Current projects

Innovation

I founded DementiAnalytics to move speech-based biomarker research toward practical measurement technologies. With clinical and engineering collaborators, I lead ongoing, IRB-approved prospective data collection in senior-living communities, with longitudinal reassessment planned to study within-person change. My work has also led to patent applications spanning speech, language, and eye-tracking approaches to cognitive assessment.

About

I am completing my Ph.D. in Industrial Engineering at Penn State, advised by Hui Yang, with graduation expected in May 2027. I previously earned an M.S. in Applied Computational Mathematics and Statistics at the University of Notre Dame and a B.S. in Applied Mathematics with honors (cum laude) from Virginia Tech, where I was also an NCAA Division I athlete. I previously worked in quantitative finance and capital markets. I bring systems engineering to questions long held by speech-language pathology and neuropsychology, and much of my work is built side by side with collaborators in those fields.
